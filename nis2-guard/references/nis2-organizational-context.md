# NIS2: organizational, governance and Spanish context

Use this when the user asks whether NIS2 applies to them, who to notify, or what
management must do. Facts that change over time (transposition, sanctions in national
law) must be checked with a search before answering.

## Scope

- **Annex I sectors (high criticality):** energy, transport, banking, financial market
  infrastructures, health, drinking water, waste water, digital infrastructure, ICT
  service management (B2B), public administration and space.
- **Annex II sectors (other critical):** postal and courier services, waste management,
  chemicals, food, manufacturing, digital providers and research.
- **Size:** as a general rule NIS2 applies to medium-sized enterprises and larger
  (50+ employees, or annual turnover and balance sheet above 10 million euros) in those
  sectors. Some entities are in scope regardless of size (for example certain DNS, TLD,
  trust service and public electronic communications providers, or sole providers of an
  essential service).
- **Essential vs important:** large entities in Annex I sectors are usually essential;
  most other in-scope entities are important. The difference matters for supervision
  (proactive vs reactive) and for the maximum fines.
- **Health data below the threshold:** a clinic, lab or health-tech company outside NIS2
  still processes special-category data under GDPR (Art. 9) and needs security
  appropriate to that risk (Art. 32), which in practice looks very similar to NIS2 Art. 21.

## The ten measures of Article 21(2)

Code only covers part of them. The organization must also have:

1. (a) Risk analysis and information system security policies.
2. (b) Incident handling.
3. (c) Business continuity: backup management, disaster recovery, crisis management.
4. (d) Supply chain security, including suppliers and service providers.
5. (e) Security in acquisition, development and maintenance, including vulnerability handling and disclosure.
6. (f) Policies to assess the effectiveness of the measures.
7. (g) Basic cyber hygiene and cybersecurity training.
8. (h) Policies on cryptography and, where appropriate, encryption.
9. (i) Human resources security, access control policies and asset management.
10. (j) MFA or continuous authentication, secured voice/video/text and emergency communications.

## Incident reporting (Article 23)

1. **Early warning** without undue delay and within **24 hours** of becoming aware of a significant incident.
2. **Incident notification** within **72 hours**, with an initial assessment of severity and impact and indicators of compromise.
3. **Final report** no later than **one month** after the notification: root cause, measures applied, cross-border impact.

## Management accountability and penalties (Articles 20, 32-34)

- Management bodies approve the risk-management measures, oversee their implementation,
  must follow training and can be held liable for infringements. For essential entities,
  national law can provide for temporary bans on exercising managerial functions.
- Maximum administrative fines in the directive: at least **10 million euros or 2%** of
  worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher) for essential entities, and at least
  **7 million euros or 1.4%** for important entities. Each Member State sets the final
  figures in its transposition law.

## Spain

- Spain missed the transposition deadline of 17 October 2024. The Council of Ministers
  approved the *Anteproyecto de Ley de Coordinación y Gobernanza de la Ciberseguridad* on
  14 January 2025; as of mid-2026 it was still going through the legislative process, and
  in July 2026 the European Commission decided to refer Spain to the Court of Justice of
  the EU for not notifying transposition. Check the current status before answering.
- Until the new law is in force, *Real Decreto-ley 12/2018* (the NIS1 transposition) and
  its implementing regulation remain the national framework.
- **CSIRTs of reference:** INCIBE-CERT for private-sector entities and citizens; CCN-CERT
  for the public sector. The draft law reorganizes national governance (including a
  National Cybersecurity Centre), so confirm the competent authority for each case.
- Other Spanish frameworks that may overlap: ENS (Esquema Nacional de Seguridad) for the
  public sector and its suppliers, GDPR/LOPDGDD with the AEPD, and DORA for financial
  entities (DORA prevails over NIS2 for ICT risk in the financial sector).
